# Published Indonesian field-service observations

**Evidence type:** published case studies by independent researchers, reviewed 28 September 2026. These are accounts of real Indonesian service businesses reported by their authors. PROOF2PAY did not interview the businesses, obtain their work orders, or run a pilot with them. No participant quote or outcome is attributed to this project.

| Case | What the published source reports | Relevance to PROOF2PAY | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| PT Putra Bajubang, AC contractor serving Pertamina in Jambi | Technicians recorded service activity on paper; administrators repeatedly cross-checked and re-entered the information. The thesis aimed to improve service record lookup and reporting. [UNAMA repository, 2022](https://repository.unama.ac.id/2644/) | A concrete Indonesian example of the technician-to-admin information handoff and duplicated checking. | No measured billing delay, missing-customer-acceptance rate, buyer intent, or tested PROOF2PAY outcome. |
| CV Pelangi Teknik, AC service business in Bantul | A published study based on observation and interviews describes WhatsApp intake, technician handoff, paper payment notes, and difficulty finding notes and warranty information when records were misplaced. [Journal Speed / Universitas BSI, 2021](https://repository.bsi.ac.id/repo/files/316490/download/Jurnal-PIW-GENAP-Sistem-Informasi-Penerimaan-Order-Service-AC-Berbasis-Desktop-%28SIPEDES%29-Pada-CV-Pelangi-Teknik.pdf) | A concrete document-traceability problem in an Indonesian AC service workflow. | Payment notes after service are not the same as pre-invoice evidence; the paper does not validate our product or willingness to pay. |
| CV Kurniatama, AC service business in Yogyakarta | Its published back-office case study says customers received total service cost and replaced-parts information but lacked detailed service information. [UKDW repository, 2016](https://repository.ukdw.ac.id/2774/) | Supports the need for a transparent service record that links work performed to supporting details. | Does not establish billing blockers, modern workflow prevalence, or a requirement for AI. |

## What changed in the product hypothesis

- The first user to interview should be the operations or administration person who receives technician records, since the Jambi and Bantul cases show that this handoff can create repeated checking or document retrieval work.
- Test whether source-linked missing-evidence alerts save time **before** invoice preparation. The cited cases establish nearby workflow problems, but they do not prove this precise delay.
- Ask for a recent anonymized service job and its actual document checklist. Never copy a customer's document into this public repository without explicit permission and redaction.

## Primary validation still required

Recruit at least three independent Indonesian field-service practitioners across technician, operations/admin, and billing roles. With informed consent, walk through a recent real job and record which artifacts are required, who checks them, how often gaps occur, and what happens when information is missing. Log counterexamples too. Until this happens, describe the evidence above as **secondary local case studies**, not interviews or a pilot performed by PROOF2PAY.
